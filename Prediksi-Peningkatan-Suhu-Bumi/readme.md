# Global Warming and Greenhouse Gases: Temperature Anomaly Prediction

## Overview

This project aims to analyze and predict the increase in Earth's surface temperature using temperature anomaly data from the Berkeley Earth dataset, specifically focusing on ice surface areas. The project explores the relationship between temperature anomalies and the concentration of greenhouse gases, including Carbon Dioxide (CO₂), Methane (CH₄), Nitrous Oxide (N₂O), and Fluorinated Gases (F-gases).

## Objectives

- **Temperature Anomaly Analysis**: Examine temperature anomalies over ice surfaces and their trends over time using data from Berkeley Earth.
- **Prediction of Temperature Increase**: Utilize Double Exponential Smoothing Brown and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models to predict future temperature anomalies.
- **Greenhouse Gas Correlation**: Analyze the relationship between temperature anomalies and the concentration of greenhouse gases using linear regression techniques.

## Methodology

1. **Data Collection**:
   - Anomaly data of surface temperatures over ice regions from Berkeley Earth.
   - Concentration data of key greenhouse gases: CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, and F-gases.

2. **Temperature Prediction**:
   - **Double Exponential Smoothing Brown**: A time series forecasting method used to smooth and predict temperature anomalies by accounting for trends.
   - **Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)**: A machine learning model that captures long-term dependencies and trends in the temperature anomaly data to provide accurate future predictions.

3. **Greenhouse Gas Correlation**:
   - **Linear Regression Analysis**: Explore and quantify the relationship between temperature anomalies and greenhouse gas concentrations to understand how these gases contribute to global warming.

## Results

The project provides predictions for future temperature anomalies based on historical data, highlighting the potential impact of greenhouse gases on global warming. It also illustrates the effectiveness of different predictive models and their application in environmental analysis.
